## Darcy pressure and cell transport

The `hydraulics` contract supports two explicit modes. `structured-cell-prescribed-darcy-v1` evaluates face flow from source-backed pressure at every ordered cell plus transverse boundary pressures. `structured-cell-darcy-pressure-solve-v1` assembles the orthogonal finite-volume conductance matrix from positive, source-backed regional permeability, dynamic viscosity, boundary pressures and impermeable faces, then solves for cell-center pressure. At least one transverse pressure boundary anchors the solve. There are no volumetric hydraulic sources.

The prescribed mode remains parseable for immutable v6 records, but v7 admission rejects it for new runs because its mesh-sized provenance array would be copied into the PostgreSQL JSON snapshot and sidecar request. New hydraulic runs use the boundary-driven solve. Both modes use the same face transmissibilities, check local incompressible volume balance, require sourced concentrations for every inflowing boundary, and pass the resulting Darcy velocity into the conservative upwind species flux. The solved pressure and reconstructed velocity components are persisted as cell fields and included in the modeled-field report. The pressure-solved mode is a restricted, steady, single-phase Darcy calculation. It does not implement bulk-to-porous interface laws, Brinkman/Navier–Stokes flow, variable/tensor permeability, membrane water transport, or a full-cell hydraulic product solver. Linear homogeneous 2D/3D cases verify the boundary-driven solve; this is mathematical software verification, not empirical validation.

## Neutral membrane partition interface

An optional `interface_partition` block declares a positive, source-backed equilibrium right/left concentration ratio for selected neutral species on an adjacent interface that touches a membrane or separator. Positive flux is oriented along increasing x. For a face with half-cell distances `h_L` and `h_R`, diffusivities `D_L` and `D_R`, area `A`, and partition ratio `K_RL`, the steady diffusive flux is `A * (c_L - c_R / K_RL) / (h_L / D_L + h_R / (K_RL * D_R))`. At equilibrium `c_R = K_RL * c_L`, this flux is zero; the same flux is added to one cell and subtracted from the other. Unlisted species keep the existing continuous-concentration law.

The neutral law is limited to neutral species in this supporting-electrolyte profile. It does not solve Donnan equilibrium, charged-ion partition, membrane convection or water transport. Input validation binds units and provenance, the equation graph records the interface and coefficient path, and the runtime version prevents an older worker from claiming the new input.

## Ideal Donnan interface for an ion-exchange membrane

An alternative `interface_partition` version, `structured-cell-ideal-donnan-partition-v1`, declares adjacent positive-x layers, one ion-exchange membrane side, a signed source-backed fixed-charge density `X` in mol/m³, and source-backed positive partition coefficients `K_i` for every charged species. At least one cation and one anion are required; absolute valence is limited to four. A porous separator is rejected because it requires a different interface law. Neutral species may be selected as ordinary partitioned solutes.

At each membrane face, the solution-side cell concentrations determine the dimensionless local Donnan jump `psi_D` from `X + sum_i(z_i K_i c_i,solution exp(-z_i psi_D)) = 0`. The sign convention is `psi_D = F (phi_membrane - phi_solution)/(R T)`, and the ideal interfacial equilibrium is `c_i,membrane = K_i c_i,solution exp(-z_i psi_D)`. The unique monotone scalar root is bracketed with log-scaled charge terms. The analytic implicit derivative of this root enters the sparse trace-species flux Jacobian.

For a positive-x face, the Scharfetter–Gummel exponent combines the continuous bulk potential difference with the oriented Donnan jump. Each side's half-cell resistance uses its declared partition factor, and the species flux is computed once and enters neighboring balances with opposite signs. The supporting-electrolyte Ohmic current uses only the continuous bulk potential and its declared fixed conductivity; the equilibrium Donnan jump is not modeled as a resistive drop. Membrane-normal convection remains rejected by the existing flow boundary, and no membrane-water law is inferred. The output records per-interface potential summaries, face count, fixed charge, and a conservative electroneutrality residual bound (maximum absolute residual divided by the minimum face charge scale). Per-species scalar reductions include source-bound valence and partition coefficient, mean solution concentration, minimum/mean/maximum predicted equilibrium membrane-side concentration, mean effective partition factor, and integrated positive-x species flux in mol/s. These are modeled local interface predictions, not measurements or solved membrane-volume concentrations; effective partition is not transport selectivity. The worker checks species identity, input coefficients, temperature-to-voltage conversion and flux binding; the residual is tied to the source-backed input hash in the result and report.

This is interface-local ideal Donnan partitioning, not full membrane-volume electroneutral Nernst–Planck closure. The bulk potential still uses fixed conductivity for an unmodeled supporting electrolyte; individual trace-ion fluxes do not determine total conductivity. No activity-coefficient model, interfacial storage, membrane water convection, swelling, fouling, or experimental validation is included. Synthetic 2D/3D tests verify the analytic monovalent Donnan limit, both interface orientations, conservative shared-face transport, local charge closure, and directional derivatives of the coupled Jacobian. These remain mathematical verification, not empirical validation or decision evidence.
